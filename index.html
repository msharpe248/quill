Mail, from the keyboard, on your Mac

Quill is a mail program for macOS built around the keyboard: a native app whose main surface is a terminal it draws itself.

Download Source on GitHub

What Quill does

  • Reads, writes, files and searches your mail with single keys, with a command palette for everything else.
  • Works with the mail accounts you already have: Gmail and Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 and Outlook.com, Exchange, and any IMAP or POP3 server.
  • Shows messages as text, or as the web pages they were sent as, with remote pictures left out until you ask for them.
  • Lets you write mail in its own editor, or in your own Vim, nano or mg, inside the message's pane.
  • Keeps your mail on your Mac, so reading and searching work without a connection.

Your mail stays yours

Quill has no servers of its own. Your mail, your passwords and your sign-ins stay on your Mac, and go nowhere but to your own mail providers. Nothing is sent to Quill's author, and Quill has no analytics, advertising or tracking. The privacy policy says exactly what Quill keeps and what it connects to.

Why Quill asks for access to your Google account

To be a mail program for a Gmail or Google Workspace account, Quill has to read that account's mail and send mail from it. When you add such an account, Google asks you to sign in and to allow Quill access to your mail. Quill uses that access for one thing: to show you your mail and to send, move, mark and delete mail as you direct, on your own Mac. Your Google data is not sent to Quill's author or to anyone else, and is not used for advertising or to train AI models. You can take the access away at any time in your Google account.
